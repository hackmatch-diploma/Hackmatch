# Evidence of the Relevance

The primary data for the project come from the team's Student Hackathon Survey, collected through Google Forms. The response summary contains 17 submissions: 13 respondents consented to participate and four declined. Questions 2 to 11 each contain 13 responses, and Q12 contains five optional text responses. The analysis uses the consenting responses. Counts and percentages below come from the corresponding question summaries, preserved as screenshots in the project repository. The questionnaire link provides the questions; the repository screenshots provide the results used in this report. For questions allowing several selections, percentages use 13 respondents as the denominator and need not sum to 100%.

In Q2, six of the 13 respondents reported no hackathon experience, three reported one event, two reported two to three events, and two reported four or more. Non-participation totals differ between Q2, Q3 and Q6. The small sample and inconsistent answers limit generalisation; question-specific totals are reported without calculating experienced-only rates.

In Q10, six respondents (46.2%) preferred choosing teammates themselves, three (23.1%) preferred multi-criteria suggestions with a final personal choice, and one (7.7%) preferred system assignment with an option to request changes. No respondent selected organiser assignment. These preferences support voluntary recommendations and participant confirmation, but do not establish that a matching algorithm improves team performance.

In Q11, registration and profiles received seven selections (53.8%), judging criteria and feedback five (38.5%), and teammate search and announcements four each (30.8%). These choices identify the functions respondents considered useful and support bringing registration, team discovery, event information and judging into one platform. Q8 also recorded two selections each for schedule changes and confusing submission, and one each for scattered information and unclear rules or judging. These are reported concerns within this sample, rather than evidence that every respondent experienced these problems.

These findings justify developing a focused HackMatch prototype with registration and profiles as core functions, visible judging criteria and feedback, shared announcements and optional teammate recommendations. Q10 supports keeping the final team decision with participants: six respondents preferred choosing teammates themselves and three preferred suggestions followed by personal choice. The proposed workflow will therefore let participants review recommendations and accept or decline them. The survey establishes priorities for implementation; the evaluation in Section 9 will test whether the resulting system is usable and whether matching meets its stated criteria.

Q7 recorded three selections for different commitment levels and two for unclear roles. These responses support making commitment preferences and team roles visible in participant profiles. Two Q12 responses suggested pre-event networking or a central platform, which supports providing a shared place for participant discovery and event activities. Q7 also contains overlapping no-difficulty and not-applicable selections, so these findings are exploratory. No respondent selected missing skills as an experienced difficulty in Q7. Complementary skills are therefore a design criterion informed by the literature in Section 3, rather than a problem demonstrated by this survey.

The reason to develop this project is the combination of these locally identified priorities with the gap discussed in Sections 3 and 4: a student hackathon workflow that includes event administration, participant profiles, explainable teammate recommendations and participant confirmation. The survey alone does not establish that existing platforms cannot meet these needs or that the proposed algorithm improves team performance. The platform comparison provides the basis for the development gap, while prototype testing and algorithm evaluation will assess the proposed solution.
